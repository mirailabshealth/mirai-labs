FOLLOW THE RESEARCH

A trial record is a starting point.

1. Confirm the exact intervention

Check ingredient identity, formulation, route, and combinations. A related analogue or parent protein may answer a different question.

2. Check the record and date

Read the study type, phase, eligibility, sponsor, status, and last update. “Completed” does not mean results are posted or a treatment works.

3. Look for outcomes and harms

Compare posted results with the protocol and publication. Check participant numbers, primary endpoints, uncertainty, adverse events, and corrections.

Each compound profile now links to a PubMed search and a ClinicalTrials.gov search. These open current external search results; they are not a curated list of verified matching trials. Review the exact intervention and linked records.

BEFORE THE HEADLINE

Five questions for every claim.

  1. Who or what was studied? Cells, animals, healthy volunteers, and patients are different populations.
  2. What was compared? Randomization, blinding, controls, and sample size affect confidence.
  3. What changed? A biomarker, symptom, clinical event, and lifespan are not interchangeable outcomes.
  4. What remains uncertain? Read absolute effects, confidence intervals, attrition, adverse events, funding, and follow-up.
  5. Does it apply to this exact compound? Identity, route, product quality, and direct combination evidence matter.
