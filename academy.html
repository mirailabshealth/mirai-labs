THE PROPOSED CURRICULUM

Nine modules.
One clearer perspective.

A suggested four-week pathway. Final lesson lengths and teaching materials are being developed.

Compound names describe planned research examples, not treatments offered or recommended.

01Peptide foundationsUnderstand the language before the claims.+

Amino acids, peptides and proteins; signaling and receptors; why a plausible mechanism does not establish a clinical benefit.

Learning outcome: Explain a mechanism and identify the evidence still needed.

02Understanding evidenceStart with the study design.+

Laboratory experiments, observational studies, randomized trials, reviews, and trial registrations.

Learning outcome: Classify a research report and describe its limits.

03Reading clinical resultsFind the result behind the headline.+

Participants, comparators, endpoints, absolute and relative effects, uncertainty, missing data, harms and funding.

Learning outcome: Write a balanced summary using a structured evidence worksheet.

04Metabolic researchKeep interventions and outcomes distinct.+

Selected GLP-1 and related research as case studies; exact formulations, study populations and combination versus single-agent findings.

Learning outcome: Identify which intervention and outcome a claim actually describes.

05Repair, skin & immune researchQuestion the leap from a model to a person.+

Selected BPC-157, TB-500, GHK-Cu and thymosin-related examples, with attention to identity, routes and human-evidence gaps.

Learning outcome: Recognize when related compounds or routes cannot be treated as equivalent.

06Hormonal, neurological & aging researchA pathway is the beginning of a question.+

Selected growth-hormone, neuropeptide and mitochondrial research; biomarkers versus patient outcomes. Related non-peptide compounds are labeled separately.

Learning outcome: Distinguish mechanistic findings from demonstrated health outcomes.

07Product quality & safety literacyKnow what a laboratory report can answer.+

Identity, purity, amount, potency, sterility, endotoxins and stability; the scope and limits of a certificate of analysis.

Learning outcome: Identify missing quality information without declaring a product safe.

08Regulation & responsible communicationExplain what is known without overstating it.+

Dated regulatory context, research labeling, investigational status, compounded-product distinctions and responsible educational wording.

Learning outcome: Separate evidence claims from authorization claims.

09Applying the methodPut your understanding to work.+

Literature searches, trial records, overlapping studies, corrections and conflicting findings. A final structured appraisal with feedback.

Learning outcome: Produce a traceable evidence summary and explain its uncertainties.

BEFORE YOU BEGIN

Your questions, answered.

Can I enroll today?

Not yet. The course is in development. There is no payment collection, student login or reservation form on this page. You can try the free sample now.

How is this different from the free library?

The learning library remains free. The planned course adds a structured sequence, guided exercises, assessments, feedback and scheduled education sessions.

Will this qualify me to treat clients?

No. The course is designed for education and personal enrichment, not clinical practice or qualification for employment. It does not expand anyone’s professional scope of practice.

Are the lessons medically reviewed?

Review of the complete course is pending. We will not describe it as clinically reviewed or accredited without the appropriate completed review or authorization.

What are the access and refund terms?

Twelve months of access is planned. Final access, support, cancellation, refund and assessment policies will be shown before enrollment. No payment is being accepted now.

Does it include treatment or a consultation?

No. Education is separate from clinical care. The course does not include medication, prescribing, a clinical assessment or access to patient records.
