This is a dated editorial review of selected primary sources. A trial result, a policy announcement and an FDA approval answer different questions. This website provides education and does not supply compounds or provide clinical care.

Recent research added to the guide

  • Retatrutide: 2026 TRIUMPH-1 and TRIUMPH-2 phase 3 results, plus the October sleep-apnea report. Nested subgroup participants are not counted as independent replication.
  • Survodutide: 2026 SYNCHRONIZE-1 and SYNCHRONIZE-2 phase 3 results in populations without and with type 2 diabetes.
  • CagriSema: REIMAGINE 2 diabetes findings alongside REDEFINE 1 obesity findings, with the linked correction notice.
  • Mazdutide: GLORY-2 and the US phase 2 trial, with differences in population, analysis and tolerability explained.
  • Semaglutide and tirzepatide: STEP UP and the direct SURMOUNT-5 comparison. Cross-trial percentages are not a substitute for a head-to-head trial.
  • Tesamorelin, GHRP-6 and NAD+: newer human findings with negative outcomes, exploratory analyses and population limits retained.

The profiles link the original publications. Study averages do not predict an individual result, and none of these summaries validates an untested preparation or combination.

October 2026 HHS peptide policy

The October 30 Federal Register notice announces a draft enforcement policy; the linked HHS document describes conditional interim enforcement discretion for specified 503A compounding involving BPC-157, epitalon, KPV, MOTS-c, Semax and TB-500.

This is not FDA drug approval or a final rule adding substances to the 503A bulks list. The document includes conditions concerning exact substance identity, qualified compounders, patient-specific prescriptions, manufacturing, testing, labeling and reporting. It specifies a single listed active ingredient; it does not establish support for GLOW, KLOW or other multi-ingredient blends. Evidence and safety uncertainties remain.

Approval and safety remain product-specific

FDA's current GLP-1 notice states that retatrutide and cagrilintide are not components of approved drugs and cannot be used in compounding under federal law. They are not among the six substances in the HHS document above.

Forzinity (elamipretide) received accelerated FDA approval in September 2025 for Barth syndrome in patients weighing at least 30 kg, based on knee-muscle strength. Confirmatory evidence of patient benefit is required. This does not establish general longevity benefit or equivalence of other materials labeled SS-31.

What this review covers

The November 1 update screened named compounds in PubMed for recent publications and checked selected original-study abstracts, linked correction notices and FDA/HHS sources. The 45 profiles are selective educational summaries, not an exhaustive systematic review. Recent search results can include unrelated substances, reviews or protocols; these are not treated as completed clinical evidence.

Profile review dates describe the sources actually appraised for that entry. Older dates remain where the scientific summary was not newly appraised. A website update does not mean every study, full text, supplementary analysis or safety signal has been reviewed. No automatic literature monitoring is running.

Human trials are distinguished from animal and cell studies. We retain negative results and uncertainty, identify the analysis when reporting trial percentages, and link sources so readers can inspect methods and corrections.

Trial registration is not proof of benefit. ClinicalTrials.gov provides protocols, study status and any posted results. Publication abstracts may not contain all methods or safety information.

For analytical testing and certificates of analysis, see Safety. Research publications are not batch test reports.
