Safety & research literacy

Read this before interpreting a result

Research dose context is literature only — not a recommendation. A publication's experimental exposure is tied to its model, formulation and methods. This resource provides no personal dosing or administration schedules.

Mechanism, marker, meaningful outcome

A receptor is a molecular target that responds to a signal. An agonist activates it; activation does not guarantee a beneficial outcome. A biomarker is a measurement such as GH, glucose or a metabolite. A clinical outcome concerns symptoms, function, disease events or survival. A change in a marker does not automatically predict the latter.

Randomization helps separate treatment effects from other differences between groups. Confidence intervals show statistical uncertainty; a small study can miss uncommon harms. A negative primary endpoint must not be replaced by a favorable exploratory subgroup without clearly identifying that change.

Identity and combination uncertainty

Fragments, analogues, salts, modifications and formulations can behave differently. CJC-1295 with DAC is not interchangeable with a no-DAC label; TB-500 is not a reliable synonym for full-length thymosin beta-4. GLOW and KLOW are not standardized molecular names.

Two plausible mechanisms do not demonstrate synergy. The combination itself needs a controlled study with an appropriate comparator. Overlapping pathways and duplicated ingredients can introduce additional uncertainty.

What analytical testing establishes

  • Identity: whether measured structural features match the claimed substance.
  • Purity: the proportion detected by a particular analytical method; this does not independently establish quantity or biological potency.
  • Quantity: a validated assay of the amount present.
  • Sterility and endotoxins: separate microbial and bacterial-toxin assessments.
  • Stability: whether attributes remain within defined limits under tested conditions.

A certificate relates to the sampled material and methods. It does not prove clinical safety, effectiveness or the quality of an untested batch.

FDA / ICH Q6B: analytical principles for biological substances

Approval is specific

A pharmaceutical approval is tied to a defined preparation and indication. It cannot be transferred to an experimental preparation. Compounding is not FDA approval. Read the dated regulatory context.

A reading checklist

Identify the exact molecule, model, sample size, comparator, duration, prespecified endpoint, missing data, adverse events and funding. Ask whether the result has been independently replicated. Read corrections and distinguish an abstract from a full methods review.
