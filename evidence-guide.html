Four questions worth asking

Why isn’t an animal result a human result?

A finding in cells or animals can help explain a mechanism and guide further research. It cannot establish that a treatment helps people.

Check whether the profile describes laboratory experiments, human participants, or both. A plausible mechanism is a question to test—not a demonstrated clinical outcome.

What does “phase 2” actually tell us?

Phase 2 studies investigate effectiveness and side effects in people with the condition being studied. Later trials usually examine benefit and harms in larger groups.

A phase label is not a quality score. Read the design, participants, comparison, duration and results. A completed trial is not the same as a positive result or regulatory approval.

Can I compare two headline percentages?

Two studies may involve different people, follow-up periods and ways of analyzing missing data. Their percentages alone cannot establish which treatment is better.

Look for a direct comparison and check what was measured. A biomarker, a symptom score and a clinical event answer different questions.

Does evidence for two ingredients prove a blend works?

A result for one ingredient does not show what happens when it is combined with another. The exact ingredients, formulation, route and study population matter.

Read direct combination findings separately from component evidence. The library’s combination notes explain gaps; they are not a compatibility check or a regimen.

EVIDENCE EXPLORER

What kind of evidence am I looking at?

Choose a study type to see the question it can address—and what still needs checking. These are general reading aids, not ratings of a particular paper.

Cells or animals

What it can tell you
Helps investigate biological mechanisms and early safety signals.
What it cannot settle
Does not establish clinical benefit in people.
Ask next
Was the exact compound studied? What would need to be tested in humans?

Observational study

What it can tell you
Examines patterns in people without randomly assigning the exposure.
What it cannot settle
An association alone does not establish cause and effect.
Ask next
Could other differences between the groups explain the result?

Randomized trial

What it can tell you
Assigns participants by chance to compare interventions.
What it cannot settle
Randomization does not remove every source of bias or establish benefit outside the studied setting.
Ask next
Check masking, missing data, primary outcomes, adverse events and follow-up.

Trial registration

What it can tell you
Describes a planned or ongoing study and may include posted results.
What it cannot settle
A listing or completed status alone is not a positive finding.
Ask next
Are results posted? Do the reported outcomes match the original plan?

Press release or conference report

What it can tell you
Can alert readers to emerging findings.
What it cannot settle
A short announcement may omit methods and uncertainties needed for appraisal.
Ask next
Find the underlying study. Is a full report available, and was it peer reviewed?
